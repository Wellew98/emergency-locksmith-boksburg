The padlock on your yard gate is probably the weakest thing you own

Mixed-use streets mean mixed-use security, and the gap is almost always the same: a serious wall, a serious gate, and a hardware-shop padlock holding the whole thing shut. Shackle cutters do not care how good the gate is.

The other one we see out here is key sprawl. A small workshop hands out keys as staff come and go, nobody keeps a list, and after five years there is no honest answer to how many keys open the front. That is a rekey, not a rebuild, and it costs far less than people expect.

If you run a yard or a workshop, the two jobs worth doing before anything goes wrong are a proper closed-shackle padlock on the gate and one rekey to reset who holds what.

Before we open anything

Before we open anything we ask for ID and something tying you to the address or the vehicle: a lease, a municipal or trading account, a licence disc, registration papers.

For business premises the owner on the phone plus a document is usually the quickest route. Sort it out before we arrive, not at the gate.
