Locksmith FAQ: Boksburg

Straight answers on what a locksmith costs in Boksburg and what actually happens on a call-out.

Most locksmith websites will not answer the price question. These are the questions people in Boksburg actually search for, answered with real rand figures. For the full breakdown by job, see the Boksburg locksmith price guide.

How much is the cheapest locksmith in Boksburg?

The cheapest work is key duplication and straightforward rekeying, which generally starts around R250. A basic call-out on its own sits between R350 and R750 depending on distance and time of day.

Be careful with quotes that sit far below those numbers. An unusually low call-out fee is sometimes the hook, and the real bill appears once the technician is at your gate and you are in no position to argue. Ask for a total figure on the phone, including the call-out, and ask what happens to the price if the lock has to be drilled rather than picked.

How much does a locksmith cost in South Africa?

Published rates across South Africa run from about R250 to R3 500. Most routine residential work lands between R400 and R1 500.

Gauteng generally sits at the higher end of that band compared with coastal cities, which is worth knowing if you are comparing a Boksburg quote against a price you read on a Cape Town website. Emergency and after-hours work is priced separately again. See the after-hours question below, because South African locksmiths do not all calculate it the same way.

How much does it cost to change a lock?

A standard door lock replacement usually runs R550 to R1 500 including the hardware. Lever count and hardware quality are what move it inside that band.

Rekeying the existing lock is cheaper at R250 to R1 200, and it is often the better choice. If the lock itself works fine and you simply want the old keys to stop opening it, rekeying gets you the same outcome for less money. That covers a move, a break-up, or a tenant leaving. Ask for both prices before you agree to a replacement.

What locks can locksmiths not open?

Very few locks are genuinely impossible. The honest distinction is between locks that can be opened without damage and locks that cannot.

High-security cylinders with anti-drill and anti-pick protection, certain safes fitted with relocking devices, and some modern vehicle immobilisers may need destructive entry or the manufacturer’s involvement. If that is where your job is heading, you should be told before any work starts, not after a hole has been drilled. We will tell you on the phone if what you are describing is likely to end in a replacement.

Why is an after-hours locksmith more expensive?

Because someone is getting out of bed, and because emergency calls get priority over booked work.

What most people do not realise is that South African locksmiths use two different pricing models for this. Some add a surcharge, typically R200 to R500 on top of the normal rate. Others treat after-hours work as a separate emergency tariff altogether, with published bands running from about R2 200 to R3 600 regardless of how simple the job is.

That is the difference between a R900 bill and a R3 000 bill for the same lockout. Always ask which model you are being quoted on. If the situation is not urgent, waiting until morning can genuinely halve the cost.

Do locksmiths charge a call-out fee even if they can’t help?

Usually yes. The call-out covers travel and time, so it is normally payable whether or not the job is completed.

Confirm this on the phone before anyone is dispatched, and ask specifically what happens if the lock turns out to need replacing rather than opening. That is the scenario where bills most often exceed what people expected.

How long does a lockout actually take?

Getting the door open is usually the quick part. Most standard residential lockouts are open within a few minutes of the technician reaching the lock.

The time you actually wait is travel time, and that depends on where you are and what else is running. We would rather give you a straight answer on the phone about how soon we can be there than publish a number on a web page that we cannot keep at two in the morning.

Will you damage my door?

Non-destructive entry is always the first approach. On most residential locks the door and the lock both survive.

Some locks cannot be opened that way: high-security cylinders, badly seized mechanisms, or a key snapped deep in the barrel. In those cases drilling may be the only route, and it means the lock is replaced afterwards. You will be told that before we start, along with what the replacement costs.

What do you need from me before you open a lock?

ID, and something that shows you are entitled to be there: a lease, a utility bill, a vehicle licence disc, or registration papers.

It is not us being awkward. A locksmith who opens doors without asking is a locksmith who will open your door for someone else. If you cannot lay hands on any of that right now, which happens often, because it is usually inside the house you are locked out of, tell us on the phone and we will work out what will do instead.

Can you make a car key if I have lost every one of them?

Usually yes. Losing the last key is more expensive than duplicating a spare, but it is not a dealership-only job.

Price depends almost entirely on the key type. A basic transponder key cut and programmed runs about R550 to R2 500. A smart, proximity or remote key on a newer or luxury vehicle sits between R2 000 and R4 950. Have the make, model and year ready when you call. That is what decides which band you are in.
