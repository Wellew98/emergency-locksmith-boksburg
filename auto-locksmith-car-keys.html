Two different jobs, two very different prices

People phone about “car keys” meaning one of two things, and they are not close in price.

A lockout is where the key exists and is on the wrong side of the glass. That is an opening job, usually the cheaper of the two, and on most vehicles it is done without touching the paint or the glass.

A lost or dead key is a replacement job. A new key has to be cut to the vehicle and, on almost anything built in the last twenty years, electronically paired to the car before it will start. That is where the price comes from. It is the programming, not the metal.

Tell us which one it is when you call, along with the make, model and year. It is the difference between an accurate number on the phone and a guess.

What we need before we drive out

  • Make, model and year. The year matters as much as the badge; the same model can change key system mid-generation.
  • Whether this is the only key. If you still have a working spare, a duplicate is usually cheaper. If it is the last one, the car may need all keys re-coded.
  • Where the car is. At home, in a shopping centre parking, on the side of a road. Underground parking with no signal changes how we plan it.
  • Whether anyone is inside. A child or an animal in a locked car goes to the front of the queue. Say so in your first sentence.

A photo of the key you still have, or of the ignition, often settles a question faster than a description. Send it on WhatsApp.

What car keys cost in Boksburg

Market ranges from published South African sources, so you can sanity-check any quote you are given:

  • Car lockout (key exists): R350 – R900
  • Car key cut and programmed: R550 – R2 500
  • Smart, proximity or remote key: R2 000 – R4 950
  • Call-out fee: R350 – R750

The spread is real and it tracks the technology. A plain cut blade with a basic chip sits at the bottom. A flip key with an integrated remote sits in the middle. A proximity key for a car that unlocks when the key is in your pocket sits at the top, because the key itself is expensive before anyone has programmed anything.

Whatever you are quoted, it is still normally well under a dealership replacement plus a tow. Compare the two before you assume the dealer is the safe option.

Transponder and remote programming, on site

Our mobile workshop carries key blanks, cutting equipment and programming tools, so cutting and coding happen where the car is standing rather than after a tow. For most makes on Boksburg roads that means a working key in your hand at the roadside, the parking bay or your driveway.

Broken key extraction is part of the same visit. A blade snapped off in the ignition or the door lock usually comes out without replacing the barrel, provided nobody has already been at it with pliers.

A few vehicles genuinely have to go to a dealer: certain newer models, and marques with locked-down security systems. You will be told that on the phone, not after a call-out fee.

Proof the car is yours

Before we open or key any vehicle we ask for ID plus the licence disc or the registration papers. If the papers are locked inside the car, say so when you call and we will work out what else will do.

It is the same check that stops somebody standing next to your car in a parking lot getting it opened while you are inside shopping.
