It is usually three points, not the whole house

People ask to “upgrade the security” and picture a large invoice. In practice, on most Boksburg properties, the work that changes anything is three points: the door you actually use, the back or kitchen door nobody thinks about, and the gate.

Internal doors, spare-room doors and the door to the garage that is already behind a locked gate are almost never worth spending on. If somebody quotes you for every door in the house, ask them to explain what each one is protecting.

Start with a walk-around, then spend on the two or three weak points. That is a far better use of the same money.

What actually makes a difference

A better cylinder

Anti-snap, anti-drill and anti-bump cylinders resist the quick methods. On a sound door this is the single highest-value change, and it usually fits the hardware you already have.

A second locking point

A deadbolt above or below the existing lock spreads the force across the frame. One lock in the middle of a door is a hinge point waiting to happen.

Closed-shackle padlocks

The most common weak link on a property with a good wall and a good gate. An exposed shackle is a bolt cutter away from open; a closed shackle is not.

The gate cylinder

Trellis and security gate locks are frequently the oldest hardware on the property and the first thing tried. Cheap to replace, disproportionately useful.

Keyed-alike sets

Front, back and gate on one key. Not strictly a security upgrade, but people stop propping doors open when the keys stop being a nuisance.

Rekeying as part of it

What upgrades cost in Boksburg

Published market ranges, per item fitted:

  • High-security lock, supplied and fitted: R1 200 – R3 500 each
  • Standard lock replacement: R550 – R1 500
  • Rekey an existing lock: R250 – R1 200
  • Call-out fee: R350 – R750

Read those as per lock, not per property. Three doors done properly at the high-security level is three times the top line, which is exactly why the walk-around comes first. It is usually one or two doors that justify the spend and the rest that do not.

What we will tell you not to buy

A high-security cylinder in a hollow-core internal door is money wasted. The door fails long before the lock does. Same for a strong lock in a frame that is soft or already split; fix the frame or the lock is decoration.

A serious deadbolt on a front door while the kitchen window has no bars and the back door still has its original 1980s lock is not an upgrade, it is a redirection. Attackers use the easy point, not the interesting one.

And there is no point upgrading anything while an unknown number of old keys are still out there. Rekey first, then upgrade what needs upgrading.

Upgrading after a break-in

If you have just been broken into, the order is: make it secure tonight, claim properly, upgrade once the claim is settled. Do not let anyone throw the damaged lock away. Insurers frequently want to see it, and they will want the police case number.

A temporary secure fix on the night and the permanent hardware a few days later is normal, and it is usually the cheaper path.
