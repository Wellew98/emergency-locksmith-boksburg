The airport problem: keys that are in another country

This is the Atlasville call nobody else writes about. Being next to an international airport means a steady stream of guest houses, self-catering units and long-stay rentals — and a steady stream of people who land at three in the morning and cannot get in.

Two versions of it. The first: a guest arrives after hours, the key safe code is wrong or the box is jammed, and the host is unreachable. The second, and the expensive one: someone flies out with the only house key or the only car key in their pocket, and the family at home is locked out for a week.

If the second one is you, do not wait for the traveller to fly back. A replacement key cut from the lock is almost always cheaper and faster than the alternatives, and for a car it is far cheaper than a tow. Tell us the make, model and year on the phone and we can tell you what is involved before we drive out.

Before we open anything

Before we open anything we ask for ID and something that shows the place or the car is yours — a lease, a municipal bill, a licence disc, registration papers. For a guest house booking, the booking confirmation and the host on the phone will do.

Say up front if you cannot produce any of it. There is usually a way through, and it is far better sorted on the phone than at your gate at 2am.
