Locked out in Boksburg? Do these three things first

Before you call anyone, walk the property. A back door, a kitchen window, a garage side door. People find a way in more often than they expect, and it costs nothing to check.

Second, do not force it. A door shoved with a shoulder turns a R400 opening job into a new door frame. A key snapped off in a cylinder is still an easy job until somebody tries to dig it out with a screwdriver.

Third, when you call, say what kind of lock it is and take a photo. A cylinder deadlock, a mortice lock in a wooden door, a trellis gate and a front door both locked. Those are three different jobs with three different prices, and knowing which one it is before we leave saves you money.

Say it first if somebody is at risk. A child or a pet shut in a car, or a person alone inside the house, changes the order we do everything in. Lead with that sentence.

What a lockout actually costs in Boksburg

These are market ranges compiled from published South African locksmith sources, not a quote. They exist so you can tell whether the number you are given on the phone is reasonable.

  • Call-out fee: R350 – R750
  • House lockout, daytime: R400 – R1 500
  • House lockout, after hours: R900 – R3 300
  • Car lockout: R350 – R900
  • Lock replacement, if the lock cannot be saved: R550 – R1 500

That after-hours range is wide for a reason, and it is the single most useful thing to understand before you dial at midnight. South African locksmiths use two different after-hours models. Some charge their normal rate plus a call-out surcharge of roughly R200 to R500. Others switch to a flat emergency tariff of R2 200 to R3 630 regardless of how simple the job turns out to be.

Same midnight lockout, R900 on one model and R3 000 on the other. So ask, in these words: “Is that your normal rate plus a call-out, or an emergency rate?” Ask it of us and ask it of anyone else you phone.

What happens when we get there

First we check you are entitled to be there. ID, and something tying you to the address or the vehicle: a lease, a municipal bill, a licence disc, registration papers. It takes a minute and it is not negotiable. A locksmith who opens doors without asking is a locksmith who will open yours for someone else.

Then the lock gets assessed rather than attacked. Most residential cylinders and most vehicles open non-destructively, which means you keep the lock you already have and you pay for the opening, not for the opening plus new hardware.

Drilling is the last option, not the first. If we get to the point where a lock has to be destroyed to open it, you will be told before it happens and told what the replacement costs, so the decision is yours rather than a surprise on the invoice.

When it cannot be opened without damage

Some jobs genuinely cannot be done cleanly, and you should hear that on the phone rather than after an hour of trying. High-security cylinders are built specifically to resist non-destructive entry. A key snapped inside a worn old lock sometimes takes the lock with it. And where a trellis gate and the front door are both locked, the gate has to be dealt with first, which is a second job.

In those cases the honest answer is that you are paying for an opening and a replacement, in the R550 to R1 500 range for a standard lock. Better to know that before we drive out.
